## Course identity and outcomes

## Detailed lab workflow

### Lab 01: Campaign objective and KPI tree to Campaign brief and brand voice

1. Read the fictional product brief; list price, contribution margin, unsupported claims and campaign constraints.

2. In mock-data.csv, distinguish observed audience evidence from synthetic assumptions and mark confidence for each segment.

3. Write one business goal, one primary conversion event, baseline, target, owner and deadline.

4. Build a three-stage journey map with customer question, evidence and CTA for every stage.

5. Audit five synthetic competitor examples by hook, format, proof and CTA; record one unmet need.

6. Create a campaign brief with approved claims, prohibited claims, voice traits, target segment and review owner.

7. Save the KPI tree, journey map and reviewed brief.

### Lab 02: Content pillar allocation to Campaign economics

1. Allocate a four-week content mix to educate, prove and offer; justify the share by journey need.

2. Use the prompt PDF to generate two drafts from the approved brief; compare each against the acceptance criteria.

3. Create a claim register with generated statement, source fact, reviewer and approval status.

4. Design a consent-based advocate request and a usage-rights record for customer material.

5. Compute allowable CPA from S$14 contribution margin and explain why ROAS alone is insufficient.

6. Save the approved prompt, rejected draft, claim register and economics sheet.

### Lab 03: Platform feasibility scoring to TikTok short-video feasibility

1. Set platform weights for audience fit, format fit, cost and team capacity; ensure weights sum to 100%.

2. Score Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and TikTok from 1–5 using written evidence and assumptions.

3. For Instagram, map Feed, Stories and Reels to the customer journey and asset requirement.

4. Specify one native-format creative adaptation for each shortlisted platform.

5. Estimate production effort and response capacity; compare with the available team hours.

6. Apply a threshold and select one pilot platform plus one reserve; save the scoring matrix.

### Lab 04: Audience overlap and exclusions to Pilot test design

1. Estimate incremental unique reach after removing overlapping audience segments.

2. Review the Singapore-local draft for price, spelling, culture and unsupported stereotypes.

3. Calculate an opportunity funnel from reachable audience, response rate and lead qualification assumptions.

4. Identify personal-data categories in the draft tracking plan; mark the consent and vendor-control needed for each.

5. State a one-variable pilot hypothesis, test period, equal-budget rule and decision threshold.

6. Save the funnel, privacy gate and pilot approval memo.

### Lab 05: AI-assisted caption writing to Creative variant matrix

1. Write three caption hooks from the approved product sheet; add benefit, evidence and CTA to each.

2. Select hashtags by topic relevance and geography, recording why each tag fits the audience.

3. Use the editable SVG marketing asset as a wireframe; define a separate AI image brief with product-fidelity constraints.

4. Storyboard a 15-second video: hook, demonstration, evidence and CTA with time per shot.

5. Build a six-cell test matrix from three hooks and two visuals; label the one variable compared in each test.

6. Reject any generated claim or visual that misrepresents the fictional product; save corrected assets.

### Lab 06: Ad campaign hierarchy to Community response triage

1. Diagram campaign, ad set and ad levels and place objective, audience, placement, budget and creative at the right level.

2. Set a synthetic daily budget, flight dates, frequency check and stop-spend guardrail.

3. Populate a four-week editorial calendar with owner, asset status and review date.

4. For a mock customer post, record consent scope, attribution and expiry before reuse.

5. Classify three mock comments as reply, private resolution or escalation; draft a human-reviewed response.

6. Save hierarchy, calendar, rights log and response decision tree without publishing a live ad.

### Lab 07: Tracking event map to Frequency and fatigue

1. Create a UTM naming convention and map ad click, landing view, lead submit and qualified lead.

2. Using mock-data.csv, calculate CTR = clicks/impressions and CPC = spend/clicks; state units.

3. Calculate CPA = spend/qualified leads and ROAS = attributed revenue/spend; compare with margin.

4. Calculate frequency = impressions/reach; compare rising frequency with declining CTR to diagnose fatigue.

5. Check consent before any pixel/event plan; validate a synthetic test conversion and avoid real customer data.

6. Save the event map, formula sheet and a data-quality note.

### Lab 08: A/B test interpretation to Organisational impact review

1. Write one A/B hypothesis, hold one variable constant and set a sample-size caveat.

2. Compare the two variants on primary conversion metric and lead quality, avoiding a decision from tiny samples.

3. Prompt AI to draft a weekly narrative from the synthetic rows; trace every claim to a cell.

4. Specify retargeting eligibility, consent window and converter suppression.

5. Allocate a small next-test budget using qualified return and a downside cap.

6. Compare baseline versus AI-assisted hours, approval quality, media outcome and review burden; save an impact memo.

## Measurement and governance

CTR = clicks ÷ impressions × 100. CPC = spend ÷ clicks. CPA = spend ÷ qualified conversions. ROAS = attributed revenue ÷ spend. Conversion rate = qualified conversions ÷ clicks × 100. Compare against margin and attribution limits, not just top-line engagement.

Obtain consent for customer data and UGC; respect image and text rights; review AI outputs for factual accuracy, local relevance and bias; keep a human approval record. Use platform tracking only when the organisation has approved its privacy and consent process.
